Flatback Turtles

Flatback turtles (also known as Australian flatbacks) are the least-studied sea turtles as well as the only sea turtle species that is native to Australia! These turtles have the most restricted geographic range of all sea turtle species, only being found in the continental shelf waters of norther Australia and adjacent areas of Papua New Gunea and Indonesia! As their name implies, flatback turtles are known for their flat shells (often a pale olive-green or gray color).

These turtles are essential to the ecosystem as their diet consist of primany benthic invertebrates such as crabs, mollusks, and other seafloor animals, and by feeding on these creatures flatback turtles keep their shallow coastal ecosystems balanced! The ecosystem also benefits from their nesting activity, as they move nutrients between ocean and land! Since these turtles are the least studied, it is essential to protect them, as we must deepen our understanding of sea turtle biology, climate-realated threats, and the conservation needs of species with restricted ranges.
