Monarch Butterflies

The monarch butterfly (also known as simply monarch or Danaus plexippus) is a milkweed butterfly in the Nymphalidae family. It is apart of the most familiar North American butterflies and an iconic pollinator. The monarch butterfly is famous for its bright orange and black wings and long diatance sesonal migration. They travel between 1,200 and 2,800 miles or more from the northeast United States, and southeast Canada to the mountain forests in central Mexico, where they find the right climate conditions to hibernate from the beginning of November to mid-March.

Milkweed is the only plant on which monarchs will lay their eggs and the only source of food for baby caterpillars. But urban planning and agricultural expansion have paved and plowed over millions of acres of milkweed.

These butterflies matter because they exhibit the most highly evolved migration pattern of any known species of butterfly or moth and perhaps any known insect.
